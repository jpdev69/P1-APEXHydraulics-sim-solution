How the numbers are built

How variable is demand for a product? Two independent noise sources combine like the two short sides of a triangle: k = √(day-to-day noise² + seasonal swing²), and σ /mo = avg monthly demand × k — σ ("sigma") is the standard measure of demand variability.

Safety stock covers demand while replenishment is on its way: safety stock = z × σ over lead time, where σ over lead time = σ /mo × √(lead-time days / 30). z = {{ result.z }} is the service-level factor the {{ result.service_level_pct }}% goal costs (from the standard bell curve — Python's NormalDist().inv_cdf).

Reorder point: expected lead-time demand + safety stock — when stock drops below it, replenishment goes out. Max stock: reorder point + one month of demand, shrunk to fit the warehouse when needed (never below the reorder point) — such rows carry a "shrunk to fit" tag.

Why one shared safety stock wins: the hubs' busy days don't sync — one hub's spike often lands on another hub's quiet day: σ_pooled = k × √(Σ hub demands²), which is smaller than adding up each hub's own sigma. One shared buffer needs less total stock for the same {{ result.service_level_pct }}% protection, and the plant also replenishes on the fast supplier lead time instead of each hub waiting on its own long one.
